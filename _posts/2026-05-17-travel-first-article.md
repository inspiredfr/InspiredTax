# Navigating SARS Travel Allowances and Deductions: A Practical Guide

Understanding travel allowances and deductions can be a complex aspect of tax compliance in South Africa. The South African Revenue Service (SARS) provides specific guidelines to ensure fair and accurate reporting for both employers and employees. This guide aims to demystify these regulations, offering practical insights into how travel allowances and deductions work, what records you need to keep, and how to maximize your tax efficiency.

## Travel Allowances vs. Travel Reimbursements

It's crucial to distinguish between a **travel allowance** and a **travel reimbursement**, as their tax implications differ significantly.

*   **Travel Allowance:** This is an amount paid by an employer to an employee for business travel, typically a fixed sum or a rate per kilometer, regardless of actual expenses incurred. A portion of this allowance is usually subject to Pay-As-You-Earn (PAYE) tax upfront.
*   **Travel Reimbursement:** This occurs when an employee incurs actual travel expenses for business purposes and is then repaid by the employer based on submitted proof (e.g., receipts). Generally, if the reimbursement is based on actual business kilometers traveled and does not exceed the SARS prescribed rate per kilometer, it is not subject to tax. However, if other compensation for the vehicle (excluding parking or toll fees) is received, this tax-free treatment may not apply.

## Understanding Travel Allowances and Deductions

When you receive a travel allowance, you can claim a deduction against it for business travel expenses. To do this, you must keep an accurate record of your travel.

### The Importance of a Logbook

A **logbook** is indispensable for substantiating your business travel claims. SARS requires a detailed record of all travel, distinguishing between business and private mileage. Key information to record includes:

*   **Odometer Readings:** Record your vehicle's odometer reading on 1 March (the start of the tax year) and at the end of the tax year.
*   **Date of Travel:** The specific date of each trip.
*   **Purpose of Travel:** A clear description of why the trip was for business.
*   **Destination:** The place you traveled to for business.
*   **Kilometers Travelled:** The distance covered for each business trip.

Without a properly maintained logbook, SARS may disallow your travel expense deductions, leading to potential tax liabilities.

### Calculating Your Deduction: The Cost Scale Method

If you receive a travel allowance, you can claim a deduction based on the actual business kilometers traveled, using a cost scale provided by SARS. This method considers the value of your vehicle and breaks down the costs into fixed costs, fuel costs, and maintenance costs. The rates are updated annually.

For the 2027 tax year (effective 1 March 2026), the rate per kilometer is determined by the following cost scale [1]:

| Value of Vehicle (R)        | Fixed Cost (R) | Fuel Cost (c/km) | Maintenance Cost (c/km) |
| :-------------------------- | :------------- | :--------------- | :---------------------- |
| Does not exceed R 115 000   | 38 344         | 132.9            | 49.1                    |
| Exceeds R 115 000 but does not exceed R 230 000 | 68 487         | 148.4            | 61.4                    |
| Exceeds R 230 000 but does not exceed R 345 000 | 98 689         | 161.2            | 67.8                    |
| Exceeds R 345 000 but does not exceed R 460 000 | 125 393        | 173.4            | 74.0                    |
| Exceeds R 460 000 but does not exceed R 575 000 | 152 097        | 185.5            | 86.9                    |
| Exceeds R 575 000 but does not exceed R 690 000 | 180 078        | 212.8            | 102.0                   |
| Exceeds R 690 000 but does not exceed R 805 000 | 208 106        | 216.5            | 114.5                   |
| Exceeds R 805 000 but does not exceed R 920 000 | 237 679        | 220.1            | 126.1                   |
| Exceeds R 920 000           | 237 679        | 220.1            | 126.9                   |

**Important Notes:**

*   The fixed cost must be reduced proportionally if the vehicle is used for business for less than a full year.
*   You cannot claim fuel costs if your employer covers the full cost of fuel.
*   You cannot claim maintenance costs if your employer covers the full cost of maintenance (e.g., through a maintenance plan).

### The Simplified Method (Deemed Rate)

If you receive a travel allowance and no other compensation for your vehicle (other than parking or toll fees), you have the option to use a simplified method. For the 2027 tax year, this **deemed rate is 495 cents per kilometer** [1]. This method simplifies calculations as you don't need to use the detailed cost scale, but you still need a logbook to prove your business mileage.

## Subsistence Allowances

In addition to travel allowances, SARS also provides for **subsistence allowances** for employees who are required to spend at least one night away from their usual place of residence for business purposes. These allowances cover meals and incidental costs.

For the 2027 tax year, the daily amounts for subsistence are [1]:

*   **Within South Africa:** R176 per day for incidental expenses only, or R570 per day for meals and incidental expenses.
*   **Outside South Africa:** Rates vary by country and currency. These rates are published by SARS in a separate schedule.

## Conclusion

Navigating SARS travel allowances and deductions requires diligent record-keeping and an understanding of the available methods for claiming expenses. By maintaining an accurate logbook and choosing the appropriate deduction method, you can ensure compliance and optimize your tax position. Always refer to the latest SARS guides and schedules for the most up-to-date information, or consult with a tax professional for personalized advice.
