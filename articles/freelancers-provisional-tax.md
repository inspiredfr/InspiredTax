## What Is Provisional Tax?

Provisional tax is not a separate tax. It is a system used by SARS to collect income tax in advance throughout the year instead of waiting until annual tax return season.

Employees usually have PAYE deducted monthly by their employers. Freelancers, however, generally do not have PAYE deducted automatically, so they are expected to estimate and pay their own tax during the year.

## Who Must Register?

You may need to register as a provisional taxpayer if you:

- Earn freelance or consulting income
- Invoice clients directly
- Operate as a sole proprietor
- Receive income without PAYE deductions
- Have multiple income sources

Even part-time freelancers may fall into the provisional tax system depending on the type and amount of income earned.

## How Provisional Tax Works

Provisional taxpayers normally make two compulsory tax payments each year:

1. **First provisional payment:** Usually due at the end of August
2. **Second provisional payment:** Usually due at the end of February

A third optional top-up payment can also be made after the tax year ends to avoid interest charges if income was underestimated.

## Estimating Your Income

SARS requires freelancers to estimate their taxable income for the year. This includes:

- Freelance income
- Consulting fees
- Contract income
- Side-business income
- Rental income (if applicable)

From this income, allowable business expenses can generally be deducted. Common deductible expenses for freelancers may include:

- Internet and phone costs
- Office expenses
- Laptop and equipment costs
- Professional software subscriptions
- Travel for business purposes
- Accounting fees

## The 27.5% s11F Retirement Contribution Limit

One of the most important deductions available to independent contractors is the s11F retirement contribution allowance. The limit is calculated as **27.5% of the greater of your remuneration or taxable income**.

**Key Points:**
- For contractors, this is based on your **taxable income**, not salary
- You can contribute up to this limit annually to approved retirement funds (pension, provident, or retirement annuity)
- This deduction significantly reduces your provisional tax liability
- Proper calculation requires understanding your income projections for the year

Many contractors leave this deduction on the table by not understanding how it applies to their situation.

## Why Good Recordkeeping Matters

Freelancers should keep proper records of:

- Invoices issued
- Bank statements
- Receipts and expenses
- Business mileage logs
- Tax certificates

Poor recordkeeping can lead to incorrect tax calculations, penalties, or problems during a SARS audit.

## Penalties for Underestimating

SARS may impose penalties and interest if a freelancer significantly underestimates taxable income, especially for the second provisional tax payment.

Conservative and realistic estimates are generally safer than aggressive underestimation.

## Final Thoughts

Provisional tax can initially seem complicated, but it is manageable with proper planning and recordkeeping. Freelancers who understand the system, maximize deductions like the s11F retirement allowance, and stay compliant can avoid unnecessary penalties and ensure smooth tax season experiences.
