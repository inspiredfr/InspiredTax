Understanding the Underestimation Penalty

In South Africa's provisional tax system, SARS imposes penalties and interest if you significantly underestimate your taxable income. This is particularly strict for the second provisional tax payment, where the underestimation penalty is calculated based on how far short your estimate falls.

This penalty system was updated for the 2026/27 tax year, with important changes to the threshold and penalty brackets.

The R1.8 Million Threshold

The key change in 2026/27 is the new underestimation penalty threshold of R1,800,000 in taxable income. This replaces the previous threshold and creates a two-tier penalty system:

Tier 1: Below R1.8m Taxable Income

For individuals earning less than R1.8 million in taxable income:

  • A more lenient underestimation allowance applies
  • Penalties are calculated at a lower rate
  • This is designed to support small business owners and solo practitioners

Tier 2: Above R1.8m Taxable Income

For individuals earning R1.8 million or more:

  • A stricter underestimation penalty applies
  • Higher penalty rates kick in
  • The margin for error is significantly reduced

Why This Matters for Your Tax Planning

If you're a provisional taxpayer earning in the range of R400,000 to R1.1 million (the sweet spot for provisional tax), you're likely below this R1.8m threshold. However:

  • If you have multiple income sources (employment + freelancing, multiple consulting contracts, rental income), your combined taxable income may push you into the higher tier
  • If your business is growing, you may cross this threshold mid-year
  • The penalty structure directly impacts how conservatively or aggressively you should estimate

How Underestimation is Calculated

Underestimation Penalty = (Underestimated Amount) × (Penalty Rate)

The underestimated amount is typically calculated as:

  • Actual taxable income minus your estimated taxable income used for provisional tax payments
  • This is determined at year-end when you file your annual return

The Two-Payment Impact

The system particularly scrutinizes the second provisional payment (due end of February):

  • If your second payment is significantly below your actual liability, penalties apply
  • SARS expects you to have refined your income estimate by this point
  • Many taxpayers make their largest penalties here due to conservative estimates

Practical Examples

Example 1: Below R1.8m Threshold

  • Estimated taxable income: R600,000
  • Actual taxable income: R700,000
  • Underestimation: R100,000
  • Penalty rate (below threshold): Lower bracket
  • Result: Moderate penalty + interest

Example 2: Above R1.8m Threshold

  • Estimated taxable income: R1.8 million
  • Actual taxable income: R2.0 million
  • Underestimation: R200,000
  • Penalty rate (above threshold): Strict bracket
  • Result: Significantly higher penalty + interest

Risk Mitigation Strategies

Conservative Estimation

  • Use historical data from prior years
  • Factor in realistic growth projections
  • Account for seasonal income variations
  • Include all income sources

Regular Monitoring

  • Track income monthly
  • Adjust your provisional estimates accordingly
  • Make use of the optional third payment to fine-tune
  • Consider adjusting your second payment upward if needed

Professional Guidance

  • Work with a tax practitioner who understands your business model
  • Have them review your estimates before submission
  • Document your estimation methodology
  • Keep evidence of your good-faith effort to estimate accurately

The Optional Third Payment

  • Available after the tax year ends (June 30)
  • Allows you to settle any remaining liability
  • Incurs minimal penalties if used to correct underestimation
  • Strongly recommended if your actual income exceeded estimates

Key Takeaways

  1. The R1.8m threshold significantly changes the penalty structure
  2. Most small to medium business owners fall below this threshold, but should verify
  3. The second provisional payment is where most penalties occur
  4. Conservative estimation paired with good records is your best protection
  5. The optional third payment is a valuable safety valve
  6. Understanding these mechanics helps you plan with confidence

Don't let penalties erode your hard-earned income. With proper estimation and documentation, you can navigate the provisional tax system smoothly.
