## The Number SARS Fills In For You

Open your February IRP6 and a figure is already waiting: the **basic amount**. It looks authoritative. It is merely mechanical — and understanding its mechanics is the difference between using it as the shelter the law built, and walking into a penalty holding it like an umbrella in a hurricane.

Paragraph 19 of the Fourth Schedule defines it: the taxable income of your **latest assessed year**, stripped of certain once-off components (taxable capital gains and lump sums — the things that shouldn't project forward), then **escalated by 8% per year** where that assessment is more than 18 months old.

Notice what feeds it: *assessed* returns. File late, and your basic amount anchors to an old year plus formula growth — one more quiet cost of the unfiled ITR12 covered here last month.

## The Safe Harbour — Read Precisely

The basic amount matters because of the role paragraph 20 gives it at the second period. When your **actual** taxable income (as eventually assessed) is at or below the **R1.8 million threshold**, no underestimation penalty applies if your February estimate was at least the lesser of 90% of actual or **the basic amount**.

Plain-language version: at or below R1.8m, estimating at the pre-populated figure is legally penalty-proof *even if your year ran far ahead of it*. That's a genuine shelter, and in one scenario it's the rational play: a year so volatile you genuinely cannot model it — estimate at the basic amount, sleep soundly about paragraph 20.

But hold the shelter up to the light:

- **It shelters you from the penalty, not the tax.** Estimate R600,000 against an actual R900,000 and no paragraph 20 penalty applies — but the R300,000 gap's tax is still owed, section 89quat interest runs on the shortfall from the effective date, and the September top-up becomes your damage-control window. The safe harbour defers pain; it doesn't cancel it.
- **It evaporates above R1.8 million.** Cross the threshold in actual taxable income and the test becomes 80%-of-actual, full stop. The pre-populated figure could be a third of your real income and it will protect nothing. High earners near the line must estimate as if the shelter doesn't exist — because a strong February, a realised gain, one unexpected invoice can push "actual" over it retrospectively.

## Where the Basic Amount Misleads

### The growth trap

Freelancers two or three years into a steep growth curve have basic amounts anchored to their startup-era income. Estimating at that figure is lawful (below R1.8m) — and quietly accumulates a large true-up that lands at assessment with interest. If your trajectory is up, the basic amount is a *minimum legality*, not a plan. Estimate honestly, use the shelter as your floor.

### The decline trap — in reverse

Income down this year? The pre-populated figure now *overstates* you, and paying on it lends SARS the difference until assessment. You're entitled to estimate below it — with your working paper ready, because SARS can query low estimates and holds the paragraph 19(3) power to increase an estimate it considers unserious. Documented decline (a lost retainer, a fallow quarter, the client's cancellation email) makes the lower estimate defensible in minutes.

### The stale-assessment distortion

The 18-month/8% mechanism means your escalation depends on filing rhythm. Assessed for 2025/26 last October? Your February 2027 estimate pre-populates from a recent year, unescalated or nearly so. Last assessed 2023/24? Formula growth has compounded a stale base into a number with no relationship to your practice. The fresher your filing, the more sane the default — another compounding return on January's filing discipline.

## A Decision Table for This February

- **Income at/below ~R1.8m, year unmodellable:** estimate at the basic amount — the shelter is built for you. Budget for the true-up and consider the September top-up to stop interest.
- **Income at/below R1.8m, year knowable:** estimate at honest actuals (cushioned above the 90% line), never below the basic amount without documented cause. You get accuracy *and* the shelter as backstop.
- **Income above or near R1.8m:** ignore the pre-populated figure entirely. Compute actual taxable income properly — capital gains, crypto, interest included — and estimate with margin above 80% of your own honest number. Your working paper is your penalty defence; the basic amount is scenery.

The basic amount is a well-designed default for the taxpayer whose life is stable, and a mismeasuring instrument for everyone mid-growth, mid-decline, or mid-windfall — which is to say, most freelancers. Know which one you are before the pre-populated number knows for you. Three weeks to the Friday-26th deadline: the estimate you compute this week is worth two computed then.
