## Two Payments, Two Different Games

Provisional tax's two compulsory payments carry the same form and utterly different stakes. August is a forecast: made half-blind, tolerated accordingly — no penalty tests the estimate itself. February is a **reckoning**: made with eleven months of the year visible, expected to land close to your actual taxable income, and enforced by the Fourth Schedule's paragraph 20 penalty when it doesn't.

If you internalise one thing about the South African provisional system, make it this: **the February estimate is the one that can genuinely hurt you.** Here's the anatomy, and the six weeks of runway you still have.

## What Period 2 Actually Settles

Mechanically, the second payment trues up the year: estimate full-year taxable income, compute the tax, subtract the August payment and any PAYE, pay the balance. Because August was half the picture at best, February typically carries the heavier load — and in a year that grew, substantially so.

This is also why February punishes poor August estimates twice: the light first payment made the second one bigger, and the bigger payment now has to be funded and estimated under penalty rules.

## Paragraph 20: The Accuracy Test

The underestimation penalty works on thresholds keyed to your **actual** taxable income as finally assessed:

### At or below R1.8 million

Your February estimate is safe if it was at least the lesser of:

- **90% of actual taxable income**, or
- **the basic amount** (your last assessed taxable income, escalated 8% per year where the assessment is over 18 months old).

The basic amount limb is the safe harbour: estimate at least the basic amount and the penalty can't touch you at this level, even if the year ran hot. That's a real shelter — but note it protects against the *penalty*, not against owing the tax with interest later.

### Above R1.8 million

The shelter disappears. Under the 2026/27 rules, once actual taxable income exceeds **R1.8 million**, the estimate must be at least **80% of actual** — the basic amount is irrelevant. High earners must genuinely know their year: a strong final quarter, a realised capital gain, a December windfall all raise "actual" and silently tighten what your February number needed to be.

Fail the applicable test and the penalty is **20% of the shortfall** in tax terms — mechanical, meaningful money, and mostly avoidable with honest arithmetic done in time. (Where an estimate was seriously calculated with due regard to the available information and not deliberately low, remission provisions exist — your dated working paper is the evidence that argument runs on.)

## The Six-Week Plan

### Weeks one and two: assemble actuals

Eleven months of real data exists. Total invoiced-and-accrued income March through January; list February's contracted work — that's your income line, nearly closed. Update deductions to actuals: expenses, home office, travel per the logbook, asset allowances, medical credits, and the s11F retirement position (contributions to date plus any planned top-up — up to 27.5% of taxable income, capped at R430,000).

Don't forget the items August estimates always miss: **capital gains realised during the year**, interest income, and crypto disposals — all part of taxable income, all counted in the paragraph 20 arithmetic.

### Week three: run the estimate against the tests

Compute estimated taxable income. Then check yourself: If you'll land at or below R1.8m — is the estimate at least the basic amount on the pre-populated IRP6? If you'll land above — does your estimate clear 80% of your own honest projection of actual, with margin for late surprises? Estimating with a small deliberate cushion above the minimum is cheap insurance; the minimums are floors, not targets.

Save the dated working paper. It's both your paragraph 20 defence and next August's starting model.

### Week four: fund it

The February payment plus any RA top-up leave your account in the same fortnight. Confirm the tax reserve covers the IRP6 figure; if there's a gap, four weeks of adjusted spending beats discovering it on the 24th. Sequence the RA contribution so the fund receives it before year-end — fund cut-offs land mid-February.

### Weeks five and six: execute early

Submit the IRP6 on eFiling, pay, and confirm clearance — remembering the calendar quirk: **28 February 2027 is a Sunday**, making **Friday 26 February** the practical deadline, and late-clearing payments attract the automatic 10% paragraph 27 penalty regardless of good intentions. Authorising in the third week of February removes the entire class of clearance risk.

## The Reframe

Freelancers who dread February treat it as a bill of unknown size arriving on a fixed date. It's the opposite: a number you can compute to within a few percent, weeks in advance, from records you already hold — with penalty rules that only bite people who didn't look.
