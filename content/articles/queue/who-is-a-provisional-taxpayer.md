## A Definition, Not a Registration

New freelancers routinely ask how to "register for provisional tax," as if it were a club with an application form. The Fourth Schedule works the other way around: **provisional taxpayer** is a definition, and in any year your income fits it, you're in — with the August and February obligations attached — regardless of what your eFiling profile currently says. The profile setting is admin; the definition is law.

That cuts both ways, and both directions matter this time of year: people who should be in and don't know it are accruing penalties; people who no longer need to be in are filing IRP6s out of habit.

## Who's In

For natural persons, the practical rule: you're a provisional taxpayer for a year if you earn income that **isn't remuneration** — amounts no employer is withholding monthly PAYE against. The usual routes in:

- **Freelance, contracting, and business income** — the core case, and everyone this site serves.
- **Rental income** from property, beyond the exclusion below.
- **Investment income** — taxable interest, foreign dividends — above the exclusion.
- **A side hustle alongside employment** — the salary is PAYE'd; the side income isn't, and it's the side income that triggers status.
- **Directors' and members' income** in certain configurations, and anyone the Commissioner specifically notifies.

The system's logic explains the boundary: PAYE collects employees' tax continuously at source. Where no one withholds — because the payer is a client, a tenant, or a bank — provisional tax is the mechanism that collects during the year instead of in one annual reckoning.

## Who's Out: The Exclusions That Do the Work

The definition would technically sweep in millions of small savers; the exclusions sweep them back out. A natural person who **does not carry on any business** is excluded if either:

- their **taxable income is at or below the tax threshold** for their age, or
- their combined taxable **interest, dividends, foreign dividends, and fixed-property rental** is **R30,000 or less** for the year.

Note the architecture: the R30,000 investment-income relief is only available to non-traders. Carry on any business — even a modest one — and you're measured without that shelter. Also excluded regardless: those whose only income *is* remuneration (ordinary employees), plus specific categories like approved PBOs and small business funding entities.

Two boundary cases worth naming. **Pensioners and living-annuity recipients**: annuity income is typically taxed at source like remuneration, but meaningful bank interest above R30,000 can still pull a retired person in — a genuine surprise to many. **The exempt-interest wrinkle**: the R30,000 test counts *taxable* amounts, so the interest exemption and tax-free savings accounts do real work in keeping small savers below the line.

## Crossing In: What To Do the Year It Starts

If 2027/28 is the year the definition catches you — first freelance clients, first tenant, first big interest year — the sequence is short:

1. **Activate provisional tax on your eFiling profile** (Tax Types) so IRP6s are issued to you. Remember this confirms status rather than creating it.
2. **Diarise 31 August and end-February** — the obligations arrive with the status, reminders or not.
3. **Start the set-aside immediately** — a quarter to a third of the non-PAYE income, from the first receipt. The August estimate is a far smaller event for someone holding the cash.
4. **Read the estimating method** — the August IRP6 wants a full-year taxable income estimate, and this site's estimation guide is the walkthrough.

The common first-year error is assuming the obligation starts "once SARS knows." It starts when the income does; the ITR12 you eventually file reveals the year's income either way, and late-registration discoveries come with the penalty history attached.

## Crossing Out: Stopping Cleanly

The reverse transition is just as real: you take a permanent role, sell the rental, wind down the practice. In a year where you genuinely no longer meet the definition — remuneration only, or investment income safely under R30,000 with no business — the IRP6 obligation falls away. Practicalities:

- **Confidence before omission.** Skip the IRP6 only when the year's facts clearly exclude you; a trickle of freelance income during your first employed year keeps you in for that year.
- **Tidy the profile** — deactivate the tax type on eFiling once genuinely out, so returns stop being issued (an issued-but-ignored IRP6 generates compliance noise you don't need).
- **The final year still finishes** — the year you exited mid-way was still a provisional year; its February payment, possible September top-up, and ITR12 all complete normally.

## The Annual Self-Test

Because status is tested year by year, the March setup fortnight is the natural moment for a 60-second re-check: Any non-remuneration income expected this year? Investment income likely past R30,000? Business activity at all? The answers place you inside or outside the system for 2027/28 — and everything else on this site follows from which side of that line you're standing on.
