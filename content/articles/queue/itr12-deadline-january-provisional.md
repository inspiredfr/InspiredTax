## The Quiet Deadline That Isn't Quiet

Provisional taxpayers get the longest filing runway SARS offers: while ordinary eFilers closed out in October, your window for the **2025/26 ITR12** runs into January. That generosity has a sharp edge — because the January date arrives with none of the reminder noise of the October rush, straight after a month when nobody was thinking about tax.

The exact date is announced each season (historically the second half of January — verify the current date on the SARS Filing Season page or your eFiling inbox). Whatever it is, this month is the month. Here's what's actually at stake, and the shortest sound path to done.

## What Non-Filing Costs

### Monthly admin penalties

Once the deadline passes, the Tax Administration Act's administrative penalty regime applies: a **fixed penalty for every month** the return stays outstanding, scaled to your taxable income — from R250 per month at the bottom to R16,000 per month at the top. It recurs until you file. A return that drifts six months costs six penalties, and SARS collects them — including, where necessary, via agent appointments on your bank account.

### Your refund stays parked

If your 2025/26 provisional payments overshot your final liability — common in a year that ended softer than the August estimate assumed — that refund exists only once the ITR12 is assessed. Every week of non-filing is a week of your own money sitting with SARS.

### Your basic amount goes stale

The assessed taxable income from this return becomes the **basic amount** on your future IRP6 forms. Leave the return unfiled and your 2027/28 provisional estimates will anchor to an assessment from years back, escalated by formula — disconnecting your provisional planning from reality and setting up either overpayment or an underestimation problem later.

### Compliance status — and the doors it opens

An outstanding return flips your SARS compliance status, which is checked in the real world more than freelancers expect: tender processes, some corporate procurement onboarding, emigration formalities, even certain financing applications pull your tax compliance status. It's a strange way to lose a client engagement.

## The Two-Evening Filing Plan

This return doesn't need a heroic weekend. Split it:

### Evening one: assemble

- **Income**: invoices and receipts for March 2025 – February 2026, reconciled to bank statements; any IRP5s from clients who withheld PAYE; IT3(b)/(c) certificates for interest and investments.
- **Deductions**: the expense summary by category — home office with its apportionment working, travel per the closed logbook, equipment allowances from the asset register, insurance, professional fees.
- **Certificates**: RA contributions for s11F, medical scheme certificate, out-of-pocket medical receipts.
- **Both 2025/26 IRP6s** with proof of payment — you'll verify SARS credited them.

If September's voluntary top-up article prompted a payment from you, that's on the list too.

### Evening two: capture and file

Work through the ITR12 wizard honestly (declare the trade income questions correctly — that's what opens the business schedules), capture income and expenses from your summary, check the pre-populated certificate data against your documents, and confirm the provisional payments appear. Review the calculation, file, save the PDF and the calculation with your records.

If a payment is missing from the assessment, don't accept a wrong balance — pull proof of payment and log the query; misallocated IRP6 payments are routine and fixable.

## If You Can't Finish in Time

File the best truthful return you can rather than missing the deadline for perfection — an honestly compiled return beats an absent one, and errors discovered later can be corrected through a request for correction. What you must not do is inflate or invent figures to get something in: an understatement carries its own, far worse penalty regime. Genuinely stuck — records destroyed, practitioner unreachable — then at minimum get professional help engaged before the deadline rather than after the penalties start.

## Then Pivot Straight to February

Filing the 2025/26 return closes last year. The **2026/27 second provisional payment** — the one with the underestimation rules attached — is due by end February, practically Friday 26 February with the 28th on a Sunday. The good news: the document pile you just assembled is 80% of the February working paper. While it's all open on your desk, refresh the current-year estimate too.
