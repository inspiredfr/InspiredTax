## Reading the Budget Like a Provisional Taxpayer

Every late February, the Minister of Finance opens the tax year's rulebook for revision. The commentary will orbit the deficit, the debt trajectory, and whatever political weather surrounds the speech. Almost none of it will be addressed to you — yet half a dozen technical lines in the annexures will quietly determine what your 2027/28 estimates, set-asides, and retirement plan should look like.

Here's the freelancer's watchlist, in order of impact — and, importantly, what *not* to react to.

## 1. The Tax Table: Adjusted, Partially Adjusted, or Frozen

The single biggest number isn't a number at all — it's whether brackets and rebates move with inflation. Full inflation adjustment keeps your real tax burden flat. A freeze (or partial adjustment) is **bracket creep**: your inflationary rate increases push more income into higher brackets, and your effective rate rises without a single announced "tax increase". Fiscally strained years lean on this because it raises billions invisibly.

What to do with the answer: recalibrate your set-aside percentage the week the tables publish. A frozen table with a 6% income increase can add a point or more to your effective rate — the kind of drift that turns a comfortable 28% set-aside into a February shortfall two years later.

## 2. The s11F Landscape

Retirement incentives get periodically tuned. Watch for movement in the **R430,000 cap** (the 27.5% is historically stable), and any structural retirement-system announcements. A cap increase mostly matters to high earners near it; the October planning article's arithmetic simply updates. Any change here also feeds directly into how much a February top-up can shelter next year.

## 3. Provisional Tax Mechanics

Rarely touched, high impact when they are: the **paragraph 20 threshold** (currently the R1.8 million line this site has covered extensively — a threshold change redraws who enjoys the basic-amount safe harbour), the interest rate machinery of s89quat (linked to the repo-driven prescribed rate, so it moves with monetary policy anyway), and any administrative modernisation of the IRP6 process itself.

## 4. VAT: The Rate and the Registration Threshold

Any VAT rate move dominates headlines and repricing conversations — vendors must handle transition rules on contracts spanning the change date. Quieter but as relevant to readers of this site: the **R1 million registration threshold**, unmoved for many years. Inflation drags more freelancers over an unadjusted threshold every year; an increase would be genuine relief for mid-sized practices hovering at the line. Registered or hovering, check both lines in the annexure.

## 5. The Assessed-Loss and Small-Business Rules

Freelancers running side ventures or considering incorporation should skim for: changes to **assessed loss** utilisation, the **Small Business Corporation** brackets under s12E, corporate rate movements, and turnover tax bands. These reshape the sole-prop-versus-company arithmetic from November's article — usually marginally, occasionally decisively.

## 6. The Cost-Base Items

Fuel and Road Accident Fund levies (effective within weeks, straight into every logbook kilometre's real cost), medical credit amounts (they feed the s6A arithmetic in your estimates), and sin/excise adjustments if they touch your cost structure. Small individually; they're the texture of next year's expense side.

## What Not to Do in Budget Week

- **Don't touch your February IRP6 because of announcements.** The payment due on Friday 26 February belongs to the closing 2026/27 year under existing rates. Budget changes are next year's problem — er, next year's *parameters*.
- **Don't restructure on speech-day reporting.** Announcements arrive as proposals with draft legislation and effective dates to follow; details shift in the legislative process. React to the published Rates Act and SARS tables, not the headline.
- **Don't skip the annexures if you're serious.** The speech is theatre; the tax proposals annexure is the actual document, published the same afternoon on Treasury's site, and it's more readable than its reputation.

## The One-Hour Post-Budget Routine

The weekend after the speech: pull the new table and rebates, recompute your expected effective rate on projected 2027/28 income, adjust the set-aside percentage, note the s11F cap for the new year's ceiling, and diarise any effective dates that touch you (VAT transition, levy dates). Update the assumptions page of your estimate working paper — the August IRP6 you'll file in six months is built on exactly these numbers, and this site's new-tax-year setup guide in early March will walk the full reset.

One hour, once a year, and the Budget stops being news and becomes what it actually is for a provisional taxpayer: a parameters file.
