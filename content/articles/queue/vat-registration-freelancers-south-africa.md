## The Threshold Is a Rolling 12 Months — Not a Tax Year

The VAT registration test catches freelancers off guard because it doesn't align with anything else in their tax calendar. You must register as a VAT vendor when your **taxable supplies exceed R1 million in any consecutive 12-month period** — measured on a rolling basis, month by month, not per tax year. There's a forward-looking limb too: a signed contract that will carry you past R1 million in the next 12 months triggers the obligation immediately.

A freelancer who invoices R85,000 a month crosses the line mid-year without any single spectacular month. If your run-rate is anywhere near R80,000–R90,000 monthly, this is a number to be watching now — the obligation to register arises within 21 days of crossing it, and trading past the threshold unregistered means SARS can treat your invoiced amounts as VAT-inclusive and claim the output tax from you retrospectively, plus penalties. That scenario — 15% carved out of income you already spent — is the single most expensive VAT mistake an independent professional can make.

## What Registration Actually Changes

### Your invoices

You charge **15% VAT** on your services, issue tax invoices meeting the formal requirements (your VAT number, the client's, the prescribed wording and detail), and the VAT you collect is never your money — it's collected on behalf of SARS.

### Your costs

You claim back input VAT on business purchases from other vendors: equipment, software subscriptions billed with SA VAT, accounting fees, fuel is a special case, and entertainment is generally denied. For a freelancer with meaningful VATable costs, input recovery takes real sting out of the output side.

### Your admin

VAT returns (typically every two months for smaller vendors) with payment on filing, bookkeeping that separates VAT from income, and record-keeping to the tax-invoice standard. This is the point where most freelancers move from spreadsheet bookkeeping to proper accounting software or an accountant — budget for that as a real cost of crossing the threshold.

### Your pricing power

The commercial impact depends entirely on who your clients are:

- **Business clients who are VAT vendors** barely notice — they claim your VAT back as input tax. Your effective price to them is unchanged.
- **Consumers and non-vendors** experience your registration as a 15% price increase, because they can't recover it. If you serve households or small unregistered businesses, the threshold effectively forces a margin conversation.

This asymmetry is the entire strategic question of VAT for freelancers.

## Voluntary Registration: The Genuine Cases

Below R1 million, you may register voluntarily once taxable supplies pass **R50,000** in 12 months. Good reasons exist:

- **Your clients are all vendors.** Corporates and agencies recover your VAT, so registration costs them nothing — while you now recover input VAT on your laptop, software, and professional fees.
- **Credibility in procurement.** Some corporate supply chains quietly prefer VAT-registered suppliers; a VAT number reads as an established business.
- **You're investing ahead of growth.** Heavy upfront equipment spend generates input claims worth capturing.

And bad ones: registering to *look* bigger while serving price-sensitive consumers, or without the bookkeeping discipline the returns demand. Deregistration later is possible but has consequences (including potential exit VAT on assets on hand), so treat voluntary registration as a one-way door for planning purposes.

## Living Just Below the Line

Some freelancers manage capacity so turnover stays under R1 million — a legitimate choice when clients are consumers and the admin doesn't fit the practice. Two cautions if that's you:

1. **Measure honestly.** The rolling-12-month test doesn't care that the tax year restarted. Keep a simple trailing-turnover tab in your bookkeeping.
2. **Don't manipulate invoicing timing to dodge it.** Delaying or splitting invoices to suppress measured turnover is the kind of arrangement anti-avoidance rules exist for, and the paper trail is visible in exactly the bank records SARS reconciles.

## Keeping VAT and Income Tax Straight

Registration doesn't change your income tax; it adds a parallel system. The discipline that keeps both clean:

- Book income **excluding VAT** — R100,000 invoiced plus R15,000 VAT is R100,000 of turnover for income tax and your IRP6 estimates, never R115,000.
- Book expenses excluding recoverable VAT, since you claimed the input back.
- Sweep collected VAT into a separate account on receipt, exactly like your income-tax set-aside. VAT owed but spent is the classic small-practice failure, and unlike provisional tax, VAT is trust money — SARS pursues it hard.

Cross the threshold deliberately, register on time, and VAT becomes routine plumbing. Cross it accidentally and discover it in an audit, and it becomes the worst letter of your freelance career. The difference is a trailing-turnover number you glance at monthly.
