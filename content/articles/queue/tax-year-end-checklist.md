FAQ: What absolutely must happen before 28 February?|Three things cannot be fixed afterwards: your IRP6 payment must clear (practically by Friday the 26th, with the 28th on a Sunday), retirement contributions must be received by the fund, and your closing odometer reading must be recorded on the day. Everything else can technically be tidied later — these three are hard deadlines.
FAQ: Why does the odometer reading on 28 February matter?|Your travel deduction divides business kilometres by total kilometres for the year. The closing odometer reading is the denominator — miss recording it and you'll be estimating a figure SARS expects to be exact, weakening the whole logbook you maintained all year.
FAQ: Should I rush purchases or invoices before year-end for tax reasons?|Mostly no. Equipment bought in the final week earns a pro-rated sliver of allowance (unless under R7,000), and delaying finished-work invoicing doesn't reliably shift accrual. The year-end actions that pay are administrative — readings, receipts, payments — not last-minute spending.

## The Week Where Tax Admin Has Hard Edges

Most tax admin is elastic — file a bit later, reconcile next month, fix it in the return. The last week of February is the exception: a handful of items either happen by Sunday the 28th or generate consequences no later effort undoes. With **28 February 2027 falling on a Sunday**, the working deadline for anything involving money movement is **Friday the 26th**.

Here's the checklist, ordered by hardness of the deadline.

## 1. The IRP6 Payment — Cleared, Not Initiated

If January's articles did their job, your second provisional payment is computed and funded. What remains is mechanical and unforgiving: submit the IRP6, pay, and have the payment **clear** by Friday the 26th. Late clearance meets the automatic 10% paragraph 27 penalty; deadline-day authorisations are how careful people pay it. If this paragraph is news, stop reading and start with the period-2 article — then come back.

## 2. The RA Contribution — At the Fund, Allocated

The s11F clock stops when the **fund receives** the money, not when you send it. Fund cut-offs have mostly passed by this week; if your lump sum is instructed but unconfirmed, chase the allocation confirmation in writing today. If you've missed the cut-off, redirect the plan calmly: the contribution lands in 2027/28's limit instead — annoying, not fatal — and next October's planning article exists precisely so this doesn't recur.

## 3. The Odometer Reading — Sunday Itself

The one action that belongs to the 28th proper: photograph your odometer. Date-stamped, both vehicles if you run two. It closes this year's logbook (the denominator of your business-use percentage) and opens next year's. Thirty seconds, irreplaceable, and forgotten every year by thousands of otherwise meticulous people. Set the phone reminder now — Sunday evening, before the braai.

## 4. Close the Logbook

With the reading captured: total the year's business kilometres, reconcile trips against your calendar for any unlogged weeks *while February memory is still warm*, compute the business percentage, and file the summary with the year's records. A logbook closed in March is contemporaneous; one reconstructed in July is the most commonly rejected document in the deduction landscape.

## 5. Sweep the Expense Records

The five-year retention system runs on completeness at year-end. One pass, one hour: photograph the receipt backlog in the cubbyhole and the laptop bag; pull February's bank statements and walk them for unlogged business spend (software renewals and annual subscriptions hide here); confirm the asset register includes the year's equipment with dates and costs; and drop the medical receipts into the s6B folder — the 7.5%-of-taxable-income threshold is computed on the full year's pool, so every slip counts or none do.

## 6. Capture the Year's Odd Items

The estimates are done, but the *return* to come will ask about everything: capital gains realised (disposal records and base costs), crypto transactions (export the exchange history now, while access is easy), interest certificates to expect, and any income that arrived outside the invoicing system. A one-page "unusual items 2026/27" note written this week will save an October afternoon of archaeology.

## 7. Invoice Completed Work — For Cash, Not for Tax

Send every invoice for finished work now. Not as a tax manoeuvre — accrual has mostly happened regardless — but because a February invoice enters March payment runs, and the freelancer's autumn cash flow (August IRP6 included) is built from exactly these receipts. While you're in the invoicing system: note your total 2026/27 turnover against the R1 million VAT threshold's rolling measure. Crossing it mid-2027 with no monitoring is an expensive surprise this checklist exists to prevent.

## 8. Write Two Numbers on One Page

Before the year psychologically closes, record: your **estimated taxable income** per the February IRP6 working paper, and your **total provisional payments plus PAYE** for the year. These two numbers are the whole skeleton of what follows — the September top-up decision, the ITR12, and the basic amount that anchors 2027/28. Where they'll diverge from final actuals, you'll want to know early; the voluntary third payment window (30 September) is the cheap correction, and it rewards the people who knew their gap by autumn.

## Then Let It End

Sunday midnight, the 2026/27 year archives itself. Monday is 1 March: new tables from the Budget, a fresh logbook page, a reset s11F ceiling, and — in ten days on this site — the full new-year setup guide. Year-ends are only stressful in proportion to what was left unrecorded during the year; if this checklist took an afternoon, your system works. If it took the whole week, that's the diagnostic — and next year's first fix.
