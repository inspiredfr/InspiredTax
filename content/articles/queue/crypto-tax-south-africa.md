## The Quiet Season Is a Good Time to Get This Right

Late December, markets thin, invoices dormant — a fitting moment for the annual reckoning many freelancers postpone: what did the crypto actually do this year, and what does SARS expect to see on the return?

The legal position is settled and has been for years. Crypto assets are not currency for tax purposes; they're assets. Gains and losses fall under ordinary income tax principles, disclosure is mandatory, and SARS collects records from exchanges and has issued targeted queries and audit letters to holders. The interesting question was never *whether* it's taxed — it's *how*, and that turns on one old distinction.

## Revenue or Capital: The Question That Sets Your Rate

South African tax law treats profits differently depending on intention:

- **Revenue gains** — profits from a scheme of profit-making, from trading — are taxed **in full** at your marginal rate, like freelance income.
- **Capital gains** — from disposing of a long-term investment — enter the CGT machinery: 40% of the net gain is included in taxable income (after the R40,000 annual exclusion), so the effective rate tops out around 18% for individuals.

Where you land depends on intention backed by conduct: frequency of trading, holding periods, how the acquisition was funded, what you said and did. The active trader flipping positions weekly is earning revenue profits at full rates. The holder who bought in 2021 and sold once this year looks capital. Mixed patterns are assessed transaction by transaction, and the burden of proving capital treatment sits with the taxpayer — SARS's opening stance toward frequent traders is revenue.

Practical implication: if you want capital treatment to be believable, your records need to show capital behaviour — long holds, infrequent disposals, no churn.

## Every Disposal Counts — Not Just Cash-Outs

The most under-appreciated rule: a **disposal** happens whenever a crypto asset leaves your hands, not only when rand hits your bank account.

- **Selling for rand** — disposal, obviously.
- **Swapping BTC for ETH** — disposal of the BTC at market value that day.
- **Paying for anything in crypto** — disposal of the coins spent.
- **Moving between your own wallets** — *not* a disposal; no change in ownership.

Each disposal needs: date, rand market value, base cost of what was disposed, resulting gain or loss. A busy year of swaps means dozens of taxable events the exchange statement won't neatly hand you in rand. Export transaction histories now, while access is easy — exchanges close, records vanish, and the five-year retention rules apply to crypto exactly as they do to invoices. For assets still held, the acquisition records prove base cost years from now; keep those for the asset's life.

## Freelancer-Specific Scenarios

### Getting paid in crypto

Accepting crypto for client work doesn't move the income offshore or off the books: the **rand market value on receipt date is trade income**, declared alongside your other freelance turnover, deductions applying as usual. That value becomes your base cost in the coins; the ride between receipt and eventual sale is a second, separate tax event. In effect you've earned income *and* immediately opened an investment position — decide deliberately whether you want that exposure or want to convert on receipt.

### Mining, staking and yield

Rewards from mining or staking are generally revenue in nature on receipt — income from productive activity, valued in rand when received — with subsequent movements handled as above. Structures vary; if this is material income, take advice rather than pattern-matching from forums.

### The provisional tax link

Here's where crypto meets this site's core subject. Crypto profits are part of **taxable income** — which means they belong in your IRP6 estimates. A large realised gain in November that never makes it into your February estimate is a classic route to a paragraph 20 underestimation penalty, particularly above the R1.8 million threshold where the estimate must track actuals closely. Realised a significant gain this year? Put it in the February working paper now, and set aside the tax alongside your ordinary reserve. Capital losses, meanwhile, offset only capital gains — they don't reduce your freelance income.

## Getting Compliant If You Haven't Been

If past years' crypto activity never reached a return, the situation is uncomfortable but manageable — in one direction only. SARS's Voluntary Disclosure Programme offers penalty relief for taxpayers who come forward **before** an audit letter arrives; once SARS initiates contact, that door narrows sharply. With exchange data flowing to SARS, "they won't know" is not a strategy. A practitioner-guided VDP application is the clean fix, and January — before filing season's provisional deadlines stack up — is a sensible moment to start it.

Crypto's tax rules aren't hostile; they're just ordinary rules applied to an asset people mentally filed as "outside the system." Move it inside the system — records, estimates, disclosure — and it's one more line on a return instead of a liability compounding quietly in a wallet.
